      How the Prediction Works

      A gradient-boosting model predicts each county's life expectancy from three socioeconomic measures only: median household income, the share of residents without health insurance, and primary care physicians per 100,000 residents. Every county's prediction comes from a model trained on the other counties (5-fold cross-validation), so no county is scored by a model that has seen it.

      The map shows the gap between actual and predicted life expectancy. Counties more than two years above or below their prediction are labeled as living longer or shorter than predicted. Health behaviors such as smoking and physical inactivity are deliberately left out of the model; each county profile instead compares them with counties in the same income decile, which hints at why a county beats or misses its prediction. These comparisons are correlations, not causes.
